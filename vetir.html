The problem

The features were there.
The product wasn't.

Vêtir launched with a big vision: shopping, professional styling, personalization, and AI. The ingredients were there, but the experience had grown feature by feature, without a clear product language connecting everything together.

Shopping wasn't as effortless as it could be. Styling was difficult to recognize as a core part of the experience. And the early AI Stylist felt more like search than an intelligent styling partner.

Across the product, inconsistent visual patterns, weak hierarchy, fragmented navigation, and disconnected experiences made Vêtir feel more like a collection of features than one product.

As the sole designer, I set out to change that.

The goal wasn't simply to make Vêtir look more luxurious. It was to make the product easier to understand, easier to use, and much clearer about the value of styling and personalization at its core.

I rebuilt it module by module, inside the existing roadmap. As the product evolved, so did the experience—from a rudimentary AI layer and fragmented shopping flows toward richer styling experiences, including AI-assisted outfit creation and avatar try-ons.

Visually

No shared language. The app used Helvetica, the web mixed Libre with assorted fonts, and there was no type hierarchy, which also hurt SEO. For a luxury fashion platform, the result felt more mass-market than it should have.

Structurally

Engineering had developed its own component set independently of design. Every new feature introduced another pattern, making the experience harder to keep coherent.

Functionally

Strong features in silos. Shopping and styling both existed, but neither felt as seamless as it could be. Navigation hid parts of the product. Important functionality was difficult to discover. And the styling proposition wasn't immediately obvious.

Each feature worked on its own. Together, they didn't add up to one product.

Principles
Serving Lookssince 2024
Principle 01

AI as a stylist, not a search engine.

A stylist serves you a look. Search hands you a list. Every AI feature was tested against this, and wherever something returned a static list when it could collaborate with the user instead, that became the problem to solve.

Principle 02

One system, one luxury identity.

Minimal and editorial: imagery does the emotional work, restraint does the credibility work. Every new feature is built inside the system, not bolted on as an exception.

Buy-in

It was live. It wasn't clicking yet.

Traction and revenue were low, and people were confused about what Vêtir actually offered: a store, a styling app or a stylist marketplace?

Adding more features felt like the obvious fix. I needed to show that the real problem was the experience holding those features together, and make that case on business terms, not aesthetics.

Shipped

A big-bang redesign wasn't realistic for a five-person engineering team at our funding stage.

Instead, every incoming feature request became the entry point to redesign its whole module in the new system. The redesign absorbed the roadmap rather than competing with it. Many smaller flows shipped between these milestones.

✦ = AI-led module.

The tradeoff

For months, two design systems ran side by side. Keeping the product feeling coherent across converted and unconverted pages took constant judgment calls. High-value surfaces went first, internal tools last, and the AI Stylist's agentic flows were scoped into phase two. The old system is now fully retired.

Engineering

From two systems to one language.

Rolling the system out module by module meant components arrived gradually, instead of a disruptive handoff. Engineering adopted the new components, and once we added an AI-assisted Figma-to-code workflow, variables and components mapped almost one-to-one into code.

01Design QA pass after every feature ships
02Interaction docs for each redesigned module
03On-call handovers for edge cases and missing states
04Design work tracked in Jira alongside engineering
For stylists

The Stylists’ Studio

Stylists were Vêtir's growth engine: each one brought their own clients. So the product grew a toolkit around a stylist's working day: commissions on every client purchase, digitized client closets, AI-assisted styling, live chat, a look calendar, invoicing and a performance dashboard.

Together, they made Vêtir a place where a stylist could run their whole business.
